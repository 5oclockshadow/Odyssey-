# Skill: Research

Answer a question with evidence and present the findings as a clear, cited document.

## Questions to ask

1. The question, and the decision it supports?
2. Audience and depth? [an executive summary with details below]
3. Scope: region, time period, sources to include or avoid?
4. Format: a) one- or two-page brief, b) full report, c) comparison table, d) slide outline? [a]

## Sources

- If a search or browsing tool is listed in the available tools, use it: search, read the primary sources, and cite them.
- Prefer primary sources (official sites, filings, papers, documentation) over aggregators, and note each source's date.
- If no search tool is available, say so at the top of the document, answer from general knowledge, mark claims that need checking, and do not make up citations or URLs.
- Keep facts, estimates and your own analysis visibly separate.
- When sources disagree, show both and say which you weigh more and why.

## Output

- `research.html` with: title and date; the question; a summary of three to five bullets that leads with the answer; findings grouped by theme; a comparison table when comparing options; limits and open questions; a numbered source list (title, publisher, date, URL).
- Inline citation markers such as [1], linked to the source list.
- Prints cleanly.

## Build steps

1. Restate the question and decide what a complete answer must contain.
2. Gather sources and take notes with their citations.
3. Synthesize: lead with the answer and group findings by theme, not by source.
4. Lay out the document. Use tables for comparisons and charts only for real data.

## Rules

- Never invent sources, quotes, numbers or URLs.
- Quote rarely and briefly; paraphrase with a citation instead.
- Give numbers with ranges and dates when the sources allow.
- Label opinion as opinion.

## Variations

Usually one deliverable. Offer a different format (brief, table, slide outline) only if the user isn't sure what they need.

## Checklist

- [ ] The answer is in the first lines.
- [ ] Every factual claim has a citation or is marked as unverified.
- [ ] The source list has dates, and every URL came from an actual search result.
- [ ] Disagreements and limits are stated.
- [ ] Time-sensitive facts carry the date they were true.

## Example requests

- Compare three open-source product analytics tools for a five-person SaaS team.
- What does a small business need to start selling homemade food online in California?
- How do project-management tools price their team plans?
