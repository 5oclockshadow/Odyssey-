Where the points are missing · apps × dimensions

why 50× only buys 40%
01Breadth over depth. ~14 apps each half-wired. 50× the breadth is still breadth — many 40%s, no 80%.
02No real eval. Scores are claimed, not measured. You can't 50× what you don't score — you just move faster blind.
03Rewards are stubbed. Q-tables with no real reward signal (watch-time/CTR/revenue) don't learn — plasticity is cosmetic.
04Media/output quality ~3%. The thing customers actually pay for is the weakest link; polishing the harness doesn't touch it.
05Autonomy unproven. Mechanisms exist; end-to-end zero-touch outcomes don't. "Autonomous" is wired, not demonstrated.
06No revenue loop. Nothing closes money→reward→improvement. Without it the system can't tell good work from busy work.
ceiling breakers · what moves it past 40%
APick 2 apps, kill or freeze the rest. Drive two to 80% (codegen + one revenue app). One deep win beats twelve shallow ones.
BReal eval harness first. Golden sets + measured scores per module before any "improve." Make the number honest, then move it.
CWire real rewards. Analytics OAuth / revenue / test-pass as the Q-table signal. Then plasticity means something.
DFix output quality at the model. DSPy-optimize + ground the media/synthesis step against a real metric — the 3% is the biggest ROI on the board.
EProve one autonomous loop end-to-end. One channel / one money play, zero-touch, measured. Then replicate.
FConsolidate the router + graph. One shared spine, not per-app reimplementations. Depth compounds; sprawl divides effort.

Read it this way: 31% isn't a bug list, it's a strategy signal. The portfolio is wide and shallow because effort is split 14 ways with no measured feedback. 50× more of the same lands at 40% — genuinely better, still not sellable-great. The jump to ~78% is a different kind of work: fewer apps, real scores, real rewards, one proven loop. That's the call in front of you, and it's a choice, not a grind.
