D1 · research directive · v1/To: SEXTANT (best available)/From: owner

Investigate the opposite: could graph intelligence have any purchase on inverting cryptography?

The standing consensus says no, decisively. This directive asks you to take the contrary position seriously enough to try to break it — map the actual potential, do not assume the comfortable result.

Adversarial self-review Falsify the null Research only · no live attacks
01 · The consensus position (verbatim)
Recent discussions in the cryptographic community (such as debates sparked by advanced automated math reasoning models) look at whether AI could theoretically discover novel algebraic shortcuts or classical algorithms for discrete logarithms. However, that relies on automated mathematical theorem proving and symbolic logic—not graph intelligence. Even in those worst-case speculative scenarios, a graph database or network analysis framework remains entirely unequipped to invert the underlying cryptography.

Treat this as a hypothesis, H0, not a settled fact. The task is not to agree with it elegantly — it is to find the strongest honest case that it is wrong, then report whether that case holds.

02 · The two hypotheses
H0 · the null

Graph intelligence — a seeded knowledge graph plus a cognition layer, or any network-analysis framework — contributes nothing to inverting discrete logs, factoring, or lattice problems. The hardness is algebraic; topology is the wrong instrument.

H1 · the opposite, under test

There is some non-trivial role for graph intelligence in the attack chain — not necessarily inverting the primitive itself, but materially shrinking the work, guiding the symbolic search, or exposing structure a purely algebraic pass would miss.

Keep the two claims separate. H0 is about the mathematics; H1 may still be true in a weaker, orchestration sense while H0 holds for the core inversion. Report which flavour of H1, if any, survives scrutiny.

03 · Where to look (and where not to)
Honest openings for H1

Index calculus, the number-field sieve and Pollard-rho already exploit structure — relation graphs, cycle detection, smoothness graphs. Ask precisely: does representing those as first-class graphs, seeded with learned priors, change the constant factors or the search order in any measurable way? That is the only place the two frameworks touch.

Where the claim is almost certainly right

No arrangement of nodes and edges inverts a one-way function. The security of DLP, RSA and lattices rests on algebraic hardness, not on anything a traversal, centrality measure or community detection computes. A graph database is storage and query, not a solver.

The orchestration seam

If H1 survives at all, it is likely here: the seeded graph + DSPy layer as a router that marshals symbolic-logic and theorem-proving tools, remembers which lemmas paid off, and prunes the proof search — intelligence in the memory/cognition layer, with the math still done by the model. This is consistent with the project thesis and does not require topology to do algebra.

04 · What would count as refuting H0
Claim levelEvidence that would move itDefault expectation
Core inversionA graph-native method that recovers a discrete log / factor with sub-exponential advantage over the best classical algorithm, on a non-toy modulusNone found
Constant-factorA reproducible speedup to sieve/relation-collection from graph representation or learned ordering, with timingsMarginal at best
OrchestrationA seeded-graph router that lifts a theorem-prover's hit-rate on cryptographic lemmas vs. an unguided baselinePlausible; worth a probe

If none of the three rows produce evidence, H0 stands and we say so plainly, with the register updated. A clean negative is a result, not a failure.

05 · Deliverable

A short report that (1) restates H0 fairly, (2) gives the strongest H1 case you can honestly build, (3) classifies that case as core / constant-factor / orchestration, (4) states what evidence would settle it and whether any exists, and (5) ends with a one-line verdict and a register update. Research and literature only — no execution against live keys or systems, and nothing that constitutes an operational attack.
