Companion A to the Seeded Substrate paper/Evidence/15 October 2026

Does the cheap brain work? The public record

The Seeded Substrate paper claims that once Bridago is seeded, quality holds from a 30B model down to an LSTM on CPU (H0). Nobody has published that result. What has been published is each of its parts: small models matching large ones on scoped tasks, structure standing in for parameters, and a market that prices efficiency. This report grades that record against the paper's hypotheses.

02 · Small models on scoped work

Most agent calls do not need a frontier model

NVIDIA researchers argue that most agentic subtasks are repetitive, scoped and non-conversational, and that small models are sufficient for them and cheaper to run. Independent results point the same way.

Phi-2 (2.7B)Commonsense reasoning and code scores on par with 30B models, about 15× fasterBelcak et al., NVIDIA

Limit: these models were fine-tuned or distilled for their task. The paper's claim is different: no task training, with the seed carrying the knowledge. The NVIDIA authors also note that large generalists keep an edge in broad language understanding.

03 · Depth in place of size

A 7M recursive network on ARC

Samsung SAIL's Tiny Recursive Model scores 44.6% on ARC-AGI-1 and 7.8% on ARC-AGI-2 with 7M parameters. Gemini 2.5 Pro scores 4.9% on ARC-AGI-2. A living survey of ARC reads it as evidence that recursive depth can compensate for parameter count. Jarvis's energy-halting loop in the paper comes from this line of work.

Caveats from follow-up work

The original results rely on heavy test-time augmentation and majority-vote ensembling, and the model uses embeddings indexed by puzzle ID (2512.11847). One independent replication reached 79.37% on Sudoku-Extreme against the paper's 87.4% (replication). TRM is trained per task family; it is not a general model.

04 · Structure and memory

The system around the model matters as much as its size

On tool-heavy benchmarks including GAIA, small multi-agent systems outperformed much larger single-agent models, even when the large models had tools. Performance tracked the orchestrator, not the sub-agents. Removing structured memory cut AIME by 18.3 points and GPQA by 8.1. This is the closest public result to Rex: memory structure carries measurable weight.

Limit: one study, and its memory is accumulated context, not a seeded hypergraph. None of these studies runs a shuffled-structure control (H5), so it is unknown whether the structure helps or just the extra tokens.

Both trends matter to the paper. A fixed level of capability gets cheaper, but the best model gets more expensive. If H0 holds, buyers get frontier-equivalent quality on their own tasks at the falling end of the curve.

06 · The market has already tested the idea once

27 January 2025

After DeepSeek released R1, Nvidia fell about 17% in one day and lost $589 billion in market value, the largest single-day loss in US market history. DeepSeek said a recent model cost $5.6 million to train. Investors read it as a sign that AI might need fewer chips and less energy.

Limits: the $5.6M figure was disputed by analysts, and Nvidia recovered about 9% the next day. The episode shows how the market prices efficiency news. It does not show that efficiency reduces total GPU demand.

07 · What remains unproven

The gap the paper's experiment closes

  • No public result runs one seeded memory across a ladder of model sizes down to a recurrent network (H0, H3).
  • No result compares a curated seed against a degree-preserving shuffle of the same seed (H5). Without that control, "the graph carries cognition" cannot be separated from "more context helps".
  • The small-model wins above rely on task training. The paper's claim is that seeding replaces it (H7).
  • Cheaper capability may raise total compute use rather than lower it. The counter-thesis has to win on quality per dollar, not on a fall in GPU demand.

The public record makes H0 plausible and the experiment worth running. It does not substitute for running it.
