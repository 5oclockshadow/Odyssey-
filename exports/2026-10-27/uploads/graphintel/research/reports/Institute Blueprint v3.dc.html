An institute staffed by the agents it studies

Every worker at Anima is a Bridago agent, and every Bridago agent is a subject of the experiment. The institute runs on what it researches, measures itself while it works, and sells what survives. This version makes it operational: who the agents are, how work moves through them, how one owner oversees the overseers, and how the output reaches buyers as the GPU build-out corrects.

01 · The premise, made literal

Workers and subjects are the same agents

Each agent is one Bridago instance: the Rex graph, the Jarvis Signature, and a substrate from the ladder. When the Theorist proposes a question on a 1–3B substrate, that round is both work and a data point for H0. The institute does not need a separate testbed; its working day is the testbed.

Every round is labelled

Role, substrate rung, seed version, seed condition. A round is a row in the results table as well as a piece of work.

Work is split across rungs

A fixed share of each role's rounds runs on a smaller rung in shadow. The comparison is free; the work was going to happen anyway.

Agents can be demoted

If a role's shadow rung matches the holder on the frozen split, the role moves down a rung. Cost falls as evidence accumulates.

The owner oversees overseers

Agents watch the work; oversight.py watches the agents; you watch one ranked list.

03 · Overseeing the overseers

A thousand small fires, seven on screen

Now in the codebase: apps/engine/oversight.py, served at GET /owner/fires, rendered by the Overseer page, covered by four tests. It gathers every source of work for you into one ranked list.

Sources

Items waiting on you, open incidents, unhealthy apps, agents gone silent or noisy, shadows that beat their holder, spend within 10% of the cap.

Ranking

Base score by kind (spend 80, incident 70, noisy agent 65, unhealthy app 60, silent agent 55, upgrade 50, shadow 45, post 30), plus one per hour waiting up to 20.

Budget

Only the top seven show as "look now", with an estimated minute count. Everything else sits in "later" with the reason. Same-kind fires batch, so one click can approve ten drafts.

05 · Funnels

Three funnels that share one top

The top of all three is the open repos and the 30B-to-LSTM chart. The stage figures below are targets to measure against, not data.

Developer
  1. Finds a repo (star)
  2. Runs it locally (clone, docker compose up)
  3. Visits the site, runs a graph query
  4. Member sign-in
  5. Pro for controls and raw tables
  6. Metered API key

Enterprise
  1. Reads the cost chart or a findings note
  2. Contact; SEXTANT qualifies
  3. Paid study (pipeline D)
  4. Design partner on a domain seed

Research
  1. Reads Plan v4 or the report
  2. Reproduces a cell from the open harness
  3. Submits a replication
  4. Joins the outside-replication programme

Measured at: reproductions, replications, citations.

Buyers are struggling to get value from AI spend, and the capacity behind that spend is financed on assumptions Gartner calls unsustainable. A correction rewards whoever delivers the same quality for less compute. Graph vendors sell storage and retrieval; small-model vendors sell compression. Nobody we found sells the claim that a seeded graph makes the model size not matter, with a public experiment behind it. That claim is the niche.

08 · Coming-out party

One hundred repos, proposed by the institute

Pipeline B's first job: the Builder proposes 100 repos, each packaging something already in the codebase or the research, and each marked open, open-core or paid API. The list, the release waves and the quality bar are in Coming Out · 100 Repos.

A drop of 100 thin repos would read as spam and cost credibility. The bar: every repo has tests in CI, a Docker run, a README that names the finding it packages, and links back to the site. Repos that do not meet it wait for a later wave.
