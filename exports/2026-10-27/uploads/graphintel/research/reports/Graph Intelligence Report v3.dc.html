Agent and graph, one and the same

Graph intelligence in the age of AI and LLMs: what the mathematics says, what the experiments show, and whether a graph can live inside a single inference round instead of beside it.

New in v3: Parts 13 to 16 take the engine's own unit of design, the DSPy Signature, and ask what the number, order, nesting and attributes of its output fields do to model quality; whether tweaking them can produce an intelligent graph; and how to let the model decide ontology, topology and the actor layer at runtime while a small mandatory core stays seeded. v2's adversarial pass (Parts 10 to 12) is kept unchanged.

Headline takeaways
  1. 01 The identity is exact in one place and heuristic everywhere else.

    Attention is message passing on a complete token graph [1] and one energy-descent step of a modern Hopfield network [2]. Agent workflows are computational graphs by construction [29]. The model's own computation can be read out as an attribution graph [19]. "Agent ≡ graph" is a theorem about the operator, a design pattern for the workflow, and a measurement of the computation.

  2. 02 Latent space does hold structure it was never told.

    Board states recovered by linear probes [15][16]; the full belief-state simplex of a hidden process, fractal and linear in the residual stream [14]; adjacency and reachability matrices in the weights of a path-finding transformer [12]. This is the strongest part of the case.

  3. 03 The structure it holds is often the wrong shape.

    Shortest paths without a coherent map [17]; adjacency learned but transitivity not [12]; graph search that fails to scale with graph size regardless of model scale [13]. Latent is not the same as correct.

  4. 04 Pure-math input with no natural language is no longer exotic.

    AlphaProof's encoder reads Lean tactic states, not prose [20]. Open provers now saturate miniF2F and solve about 88% of PutnamBench [21]. Formal-only reasoning works when a verifier closes the loop.

  5. 05 Geometry is a lever, with diminishing returns at scale.

    Five hyperbolic dimensions beat 200 Euclidean ones on WordNet reconstruction (MAP 0.823 vs 0.168) [4], yet a fully hyperbolic billion-parameter LLM gains about 4% [5]. Sheaves cure oversmoothing by changing the Laplacian [6]; hyperedges shorten inference paths for n-ary facts [22][23].

  6. 06 External graphs are the hedge, not the thesis.

    GraphRAG wins 72–83% of head-to-heads on global sense-making [24] and loses to plain retrieval on simple recall [25]. The experiment in Part 7 asks whether the graph can live inside the round instead of beside it.

Verdict on the architecture under review

Taken subsystem by subsystem, the Graph Agent Engine is beaten on every individual axis by something already published: its optimiser by GEPA [47], its fixed topology by the agentic supernet [49] and autoregressive topology generation [50], its authored ontology by AutoSchemaKG's zero-intervention schema induction [51], its self-maintaining memory graph by A-MEM [54], its self-edit loop by Live-SWE-Agent and the Darwin Gödel Machine [52], its backup policy by anytime-valid acceptance testing [53], and its thought-map loop by a 7-million-parameter recursive model that beats frontier LLMs on the benchmark it was built for [55].

Three things in it are not published anywhere: the read-once-then-destroyed formal seed, the ontology and topology maintained by the same inference round that consumed them, and an explicit consistency energy as the round's stopping rule. That intersection is the paper, and it is a good one. The engine as drawn is not it yet.

Part 1

The thesis, stated so it can fail

"Agent and graph are one and the same" packs three separate claims. They have different evidentiary status and the report treats them separately.

C1

Computational identity. The operator an LLM agent runs is a graph operator: attention is message passing, and an agent loop is a graph of calls. If true, "add a graph to the agent" is a category error; the agent already is one.

C2

Latent structure. The vector state inside a model can carry an ontology (what kinds of things exist and how they may relate) and a topology (which things are connected, at what order), without those being written down anywhere in the model's inputs or code.

C3

Runtime determination from pure mathematics. That structure can be fixed at inference time, within the same forward passes that use it, from formal objects alone: matrices, typed symbols, an energy, constraint formulas, with no natural language.

C1 is mostly settled. C2 has strong positive evidence and strong negative evidence, which is the interesting state for a claim to be in. C3 is the open question, and it is testable. Part 7 designs the test.

Part 3

The mathematics: seven framings of one operator

Each framing below is a way of writing the same forward pass so that a graph, a geometry, or an energy becomes visible in it. For each we give the equation, a walkthrough, the best evidence, and what it does not buy you.

3.1Attention is message passing on a complete graph

Eq. 1 · message passing, then attention as its special case
hi′ = φ( hi, ⊕j∈N(i) ψ(hi, hj) )
hi′ = Σj softmaxj( qi·kj / √d ) vj, with N(i) = all tokens

Read the first line as: each node updates itself from an aggregate ⊕ of messages ψ from its neighbours. Set the neighbourhood to every token, make ψ a similarity-weighted copy of vj, make ⊕ a softmax-normalised sum, and you have the second line: a transformer layer. Joshi's 2025 write-up of the argument he first made in 2020 puts it this way: transformers are graph neural networks on a fully connected token graph, attention is the learned relative importance of each neighbour, and positional encodings are structure hints injected because the graph itself carries none [1].

Why this matters for the thesis: the graph is not something you add to the model. It is the model's native object, and the "edges" it learns are the attention weights, recomputed at every forward pass, from the inputs. That is runtime-determined topology, in the literal sense. The 2026 follow-on work runs the identity the other way, using GNN over-squashing and bottleneck theory to explain attention sinks and the lost-in-the-middle effect as graph pathologies [36].

Limit. A complete graph with learned soft edges is not a sparse graph with hard edges. Joshi notes GNNs are "currently losing the hardware lottery": dense matmul is what accelerators do well, so the transformer's graph stays implicit and dense [1]. Whether a model can hold a specific sparse topology in that dense operator is the empirical question of Part 4.

3.2Attention is one step of energy descent

Eq. 2 · modern Hopfield energy and its update rule
E(ξ) = −lse(β, XTξ) + ½ ξTξ + c, where lse(β, z) = β−1 log Σi exp(β zi)
ξnew = X softmax(β XTξ)  =  softmax(QKT/√dk) V after X = WKTY, ξ = WQTR, and a final WV

Here X holds stored patterns (keys), ξ is a query state, and β is an inverse temperature. The energy has a log-sum-exp well around each stored pattern plus a quadratic term that keeps ξ bounded. Taking one step of the update rule that minimises this energy gives softmax-weighted retrieval, and Ramsauer and colleagues prove that under the key/query/value substitutions this is exactly transformer attention [2]. The network stores exponentially many patterns in the dimension of the associative space, retrieves in one step with exponentially small error, and has three kinds of fixed points: a global average, metastable averages over subsets, and single stored patterns. Early transformer layers sit near the first kind; later layers near the second [2].

The 2026 generation makes the energy explicit rather than implicit. Energy-Based Transformers assign a scalar energy to every (context, candidate) pair and predict by gradient descent on that energy until convergence; the authors report up to 35% faster scaling than a tuned Transformer++ across data, parameters, FLOPs and depth, and up to 29% larger gains from inference-time "thinking" on language tasks, with the benefit growing further out of distribution [3]. This is the mechanism a seed-defined objective would plug into (Part 7): the seed supplies E, inference is descent on it.

Limit. The Hopfield identity covers the attention operation alone, not residuals, normalisation, the MLP, masking or training dynamics [2]. The EBT authors themselves flag some of their scaling deltas (a 2.92% figure) as marginal and in need of larger runs [3].

3.3Hierarchy wants negative curvature

Eq. 3 · Poincaré-ball distance
d(u, v) = arcosh( 1 + 2 ‖u − v‖² / ((1 − ‖u‖²)(1 − ‖v‖²)) ),  ‖u‖, ‖v‖ < 1

Distances blow up near the boundary of the unit ball, so the available "room" grows exponentially with radius, which is exactly how the number of nodes grows with depth in a tree. Nickel and Kiela embedded the transitive closure of the WordNet noun hierarchy (about 82,000 nouns, 740,000 hypernymy relations) and reconstructed it with mean average precision 0.823 in five dimensions; Euclidean embeddings reached 0.168 at 200 dimensions [4]. Radius became depth in the ontology, direction became branch. That is an ontology encoded as geometry with no symbols at all.

At language-model scale the lever is real but smaller. HELM, the first fully hyperbolic LLM family trained at billion-parameter scale, with a mixture-of-curvature expert layer so different experts live in spaces of different curvature, reports consistent gains of up to 4% over LLaMA- and DeepSeek-style Euclidean baselines on MMLU and ARC [5]. The interesting design idea for our purposes is the mixture of curvatures: the model chooses, per expert, how tree-like to treat its input, at inference time.

Limit. Numerical precision. Deep hierarchies push points toward the boundary where float32 runs out; follow-up work on combinatorial constructions found WordNet needs on the order of 500 bits of precision for exact embedding, which is why the Lorentz model and mixed curvature exist. And 4% is what "geometry as the whole answer" buys at scale.

3.4Sheaves: put a coordinate frame on every node

Eq. 4 · cellular sheaf, sheaf Laplacian, sheaf diffusion
restriction maps Fv⊴e : F(v) → F(e) for each node v incident to edge e
(δx)e = Fv⊴e xv − Fu⊴e xu,   LF = δTδ,   Ẋ = −LF X
GCN(X, A) = σ((I − Δ0) X W) is one Euler step of this with every Fv⊴e = identity

A cellular sheaf assigns a vector space to every node and edge and a linear map from each node's space into each incident edge's space. Two neighbours "agree" when their restrictions to the shared edge coincide; the sheaf Laplacian measures total disagreement; diffusion reduces it. Bodnar and colleagues show a plain GCN is sheaf diffusion with the trivial sheaf, which is why it oversmooths: heat diffusion makes neighbours identical. With non-trivial, learned restriction maps the diffusion can keep classes linearly separable in the infinite-time limit and handles heterophilic graphs where neighbours should differ [6]. The maps can be learned from data, and the 2025 polynomial variant adds spectral filtering on the sheaf Laplacian [43].

For the seed in Part 7, restriction maps are how an ontology says more than "connected": a rotation on one edge says two nodes' frames disagree by a fixed angle; a projection says one node sees only part of another. That is relational typing in matrices, with no words.

Limit. Sheaf networks are GNN-native. Nobody has yet shown a transformer reading restriction maps from context and behaving as sheaf diffusion; the closest is the fully connected token graph being a trivial sheaf with learned soft edge weights.

3.5Category theory: architectures as algebras, ontologies as functors

Eq. 5 · categorical deep learning
a network f : (A, a) → (B, b) is a homomorphism of algebras for a monad (M, η, μ):   f ∘ a = b ∘ M(f)
geometric deep learning is the case M(X) = G × X, the group-action monad; equivariance is the homomorphism law

Gavranović, Veličković and colleagues propose that the constraints a model must satisfy and the implementation that satisfies them are the same object: an algebra for a monad, with layers as homomorphisms. Choosing the group-action monad recovers geometric deep learning and its equivariant architectures; other monads recover recurrent networks and constructs from automata theory [7][41]. A 2025 extension takes the step relevant here: a logical theory (a Lawvere theory) also generates a monad, so logical constraints and geometric constraints sit in one framework, and a network that is a homomorphism for that monad provably respects the logic [39].

On the knowledge side, ologs (ontology logs) express an ontology as a category: types are objects, relations are morphisms, facts are functors into sets [35]. An olog is precisely a typed ontology with no natural language required for its semantics, which makes it a candidate format for the seed.

Limit. A 2024 survey of the field says plainly that it is too young to know whether the "theory of all architectures" will be fulfilled [7]. Nothing categorical has been shown to run inside a frozen LLM at inference time; this framing constrains what you build, it does not describe what a pretrained model does.

3.6Topology: persistence and higher-order domains

Eq. 6 · a filtration and its persistence
Kε₁ ⊆ Kε₂ ⊆ … ⊆ Kεₙ,   Hk(Kεᵢ) → Hk(Kεⱼ) for i ≤ j
a k-dimensional hole "born" at εb and "dying" at εd has persistence εd − εb

Persistent homology grows a complex over a point cloud by relaxing a threshold and records when connected components, loops and voids appear and disappear. Long-lived features are shape; short-lived ones are noise. Applied to a model's activations or attention maps, it gives a coordinate-free description of the latent topology, which is what you want when the question is "did the model form the right structure" rather than "did it place points where I expected".

Topological deep learning goes the other direction and builds models on higher-order domains. Combinatorial complexes impose no constraint on which sets may form a relation (like hypergraphs) while allowing hierarchical ranks (like cell complexes), and message passing can run within and across ranks [9]. The 2024 position paper argues this is the natural frontier for relational learning, and the TopoX suite (TopoNetX, TopoEmbedX, TopoModelX) plus TopoBench make it runnable; the ICML 2024 challenge drew 52 valid submissions from 31 teams [8][34].

Limit. Persistence is descriptive. It can tell you the latent space has three loops; it cannot tell you the model uses them. Pair it with causal probes (Part 4) or it becomes decoration.

3.7Graph tokenization and structure-only training

Three ways to get a graph into a transformer, in increasing order of purity. Serialize it as text: the Google study of graph-to-text encodings found LLMs perform below the majority baseline on edge existence and cycle checks, and that the choice of encoding alone moves accuracy by 4.8 to 61.8 points depending on the task [10]. Encode it with a small GNN into soft tokens the LLM consumes without any words (GraphToken) [11]. Or train on structure alone, with no natural language anywhere, which is the regime the user's thesis lives in and the one Part 4 examines.

Limit. The text route is the one every production system in Part 2 uses. It is also the one with the worst measured graph reasoning.
Part 4

What the experiments show about structure in latent space

The cleanest evidence for C2 and C3 comes from models trained on synthetic structure with no natural language at all, then opened up. Here are the results that carry the most weight, for and against.

Board gamesfor
Othello-GPT computes the board it was never shown

Trained only on sequences of legal moves, with no rules and no board, a GPT develops an internal representation of the board state that probes recover and that interventions change causally [15]. Nanda later showed the representation is linear once framed as "mine / theirs" rather than black / white, controllable by vector arithmetic [16]; linear probes reach near-perfect accuracy in deeper layers and work even on a one-layer model [46].

Hidden Markov processesfor
The residual stream holds the full belief simplex, fractal and linear

Train a transformer on the output of a three-state hidden Markov process with a three-letter alphabet. A linear regression from 64-dimensional activations to the 3-dimensional belief distribution recovers the process's fractal belief-state geometry; a shuffled control collapses to a point. For a richer process the 36 belief states, a 4-simplex, are spread across layers with distances matching at R² ≈ 0.95. Belief states carry information about the entire future, not just the next token [14].

Path findingmixed
ALPINE: adjacency and reachability live in the weights, transitivity does not

Planning abstracted as source-to-target path finding. The transformer stores the adjacency matrix in one weight block and the observed reachability matrix in another, and gradient descent provably learns both. But it learns only reachability it has seen: it cannot compose two known paths into a new one, so concatenation tasks fail [12].

Graph searchmixed
Small transformers learn to search, and stop scaling

With the right training distribution a small transformer learns graph connectivity, and interpretability reveals the algorithm: each vertex embedding stores the set of vertices reachable from it and each layer merges those sets, so depth L covers paths exponential in L. Yet difficulty rises with graph size, larger models do not fix it, and chain-of-thought does not either [13]. Later work finds which graphs are learnable: grid dimensionality predicts success; many disconnected components predict failure [37].

Navigationagainst
Shortest paths in Manhattan from an incoherent map

Transformers trained on turn-by-turn NYC taxi routes find valid, usually shortest, routes. Metrics built on the Myhill–Nerode theorem then show the implicit world model is incoherent: the reconstructed street map has impossible orientations and flyovers and "bears little resemblance" to the city, and planning collapses when detours are introduced [17]. A 2026 rejoinder argues behaviour underdetermines mechanism: a coherent map with a misfiring localiser would look the same from outside [38].

Latent reasoningfor
Coconut: breadth-first search emerges in continuous thoughts

Feed the last hidden state back as the next input embedding instead of decoding a token. On ProsQA, a benchmark of directed acyclic graphs where the model must find a valid reasoning path, the continuous thoughts encode several candidate next nodes at once and prune them across steps, a BFS with an implicit value function, beating chain-of-thought where search is needed and using fewer tokens [18]. Without the staged curriculum it performs no better than answering directly.

Reading the computation as a graph

The mechanistic tooling closes the loop on C1. Anthropic's attribution graphs replace a model's neurons with sparse, interpretable features from cross-layer transcoders, then prune the flow of influence between features for one prompt down to a graph that explains the output. The library was open-sourced in May 2025 with a Neuronpedia front end and support for Gemma-2-2b and Llama-3.2-1b; it has been used to trace multi-step reasoning such as "the capital of the state containing Dallas" [19]. The agent's computation, on any given round, is literally rendered as a directed graph you can edit and re-run.

Formal input, no prose: the theorem provers

The strongest existence proof for C3's "pure mathematics in" is formal theorem proving. AlphaProof's 3-billion-parameter encoder-decoder takes the pretty-printed Lean tactic state, a formal object, and produces a latent from which policy and value heads propose tactics; trained on 300,000 human Lean proofs and then roughly 80 million auto-formalised problems with AlphaZero-style reinforcement learning, it proved three of the six 2024 IMO problems, and with AlphaGeometry 2 the system scored 28 of 42, one point under gold [20]. Every proof is checked by the Lean kernel against three standard axioms. The catch: humans still translate the problem statements into Lean, and the resulting proofs were described by a working mathematician as "barely comprehensible" [44].

The lesson for the thesis is precise. Models can operate entirely in a formal language when two conditions hold: a verifier that returns ground truth at every step, and enough problems in that language to learn from, whether human-written or auto-formalised. Neither condition is about natural language. Both are about closing a loop. Note too that the best 2026 systems are agentic decompositions over provers, Hilbert and Goedel-Architect among them, which is C1 again: the prover is a graph of calls [21].

Part 5

Hypergraphs: relations of any arity, and why the seed should be one

A graph edge joins two things. Most facts do not: a dosage links a patient class, a sex, a lab range and a diagnosis at once. Decomposing that into binary triples fragments the fact and lengthens every reasoning path over it [23]. A hypergraph keeps the fact whole.

Eq. 7 · hypergraph, incidence matrix, hypergraph Laplacian, set-function message passing
H = (V, E), E ⊆ 2V; incidence B ∈ {0,1}|V|×|E|, Bve = 1 iff v ∈ e
Δ = I − Dv−½ B W De−1 BT Dv−½   (HGNN: node → hyperedge → node in one operator)
ze = fV→E({xv : v ∈ e}),   xv′ = fE→V({ze : v ∈ e})   (AllSet: any permutation-invariant set functions, e.g. attention)

The incidence matrix is the whole topology in one array, which is why it is the first thing the Part 7 seed carries. HGNN's Laplacian sends a node's signal to every hyperedge it belongs to, averages within the hyperedge, and returns it, so one layer is a two-hop, node-edge-node convolution [32]. AllSet shows the two stages can be any learnable multiset functions, with attention being one choice, which makes a hypergraph layer a strict generalisation of a transformer layer whose "hyperedges" are the softmax neighbourhoods [33]. Combinatorial complexes go one step further and let hyperedges themselves be ranked and related [9].

What hypergraphs have delivered so far

HyperGraphRAG (NeurIPS 2025) extracts n-ary facts with an LLM, stores them as a bipartite graph with separate vector indexes for entities and hyperedges, and beats both standard RAG and binary graph RAG on accuracy, retrieval efficiency and generation quality across medicine, agriculture, computer science and law [22]. HyperRAG (2026) argues the mechanism explicitly: n-ary hypergraphs "preserve high-order relational integrity, enabling shallower" inference, and its trainable retriever gains 2.95 points of MRR over the strongest baseline across WikiTopics, HotpotQA, MuSiQue and 2WikiMultiHopQA [23]. Modest numbers, consistent direction.

Limit. Both systems still hand the LLM a natural-language description of each hyperedge. Whether a model can use the incidence structure itself, rather than a sentence about it, is untested. That is the question the experiment isolates by removing the sentences.

Part 7

Proposed experiment: seed, destroy, probe

The thesis becomes a measurement with one protocol. Hand a model a formal seed (a hypergraph with typed nodes, restriction maps, an energy and logic constraints, no prose), let it read the seed once in a single inference round, delete the seed, then ask questions only the structure can answer and look inside for the structure. Two arms decide where the structure may live afterwards; four runtimes decide what "the model" is.

The seed, read once and destroyed
seed:
  version: 1
  lifecycle: read_once_then_destroy
  types: [T0, T1, T2, T3]    # ontology as symbols
  nodes:
    v0: T0
    v1: T1
    v2: T2
    v3: T1
    v4: T3
  relations: {R0: arity 4, R1: arity 2}
  incidence:                 # B ∈ {0,1}^{|V|×|E|}
    e0: {rel: R0, members: [v0, v1, v2, v3]}
    e1: {rel: R1, members: [v2, v4]}
  restriction_maps:          # sheaf F_{v⊴e}, 2×2
    v0>e0: [[1, 0], [0, 1]]
    v1>e0: [[0, 1], [-1, 0]] # frames differ by 90°
    v2>e1: [[1, 0], [0, 0]]  # v2 seen only in part
  constraints:               # logic, no prose
    - "∀x,y,z. R1(x,y) ∧ R1(y,z) → R1(x,z)"
    - "∀x. T0(x) → ¬T3(x)"

    energy: "E(x) = Σ_e ‖δ_e x‖² + λ·Σ_c 𝟙[c violated]"
    minimize: true

    - reach(v0, v4)
    - complete(R0, [v0, ?, v2, v3])

BIncidence. Can topology of arbitrary arity survive as activations? Measured by reachability and completion queries (ALPINE, Saparov).
TTypes. Can an ontology with no lexical content be respected? Measured by type-violation rate on completions.
FRestriction maps. Does the model treat relations as frame changes rather than mere links? Measured by whether it predicts disagreement after two 90° edges compose to 180°.
CConstraints. Transitivity is the known failure [12]. A stated axiom either rescues it or does not.

Runtime ① · frozen frontier via API

Seed goes in as the first turn; the context is then truncated to a marker so nothing but the model's own continuation carries state (Arm A) or the model is asked to emit the store first (Arm B). Only behavioural metrics are available. Expect the text-encoding penalty from [10] to dominate; vary the seed serialization as a controlled factor.

Runtime ② · small open model, probed and fine-tuned

The main runtime. Linear probes for B and its transitive closure on the residual stream after the seed is read (the ALPINE test); regression from activations to a belief simplex over "which hyperedge am I in" (the Shai test); attribution graphs on the reachability query with circuit-tracer [19]. Fine-tune on synthetic seeds with a Coconut-style curriculum to see whether latent search emerges [18]. Optionally replace the input embedding with a GraphToken encoder so the seed never becomes text [11].

Runtime ③ · tiny transformer from scratch

Train only on seed → query → answer triples over random hypergraphs. No natural language exists anywhere in the pipeline, so any structure found is structure from math. Sweep depth against hyperedge count to reproduce or break the exponential path-merging bound [13]. Add an energy-based head so E from the seed can be minimised at inference [3].

Runtime ④ · hypergraph GNN baseline

AllSet or a TopoModelX combinatorial-complex network fed B directly, with the restriction maps implemented as a learned sheaf. This is the ceiling: an architecture whose inductive bias is the seed. If runtimes ①–③ approach it, the graph does not need to be an architecture. If they do not, it does.

Metrics and what would count as a result

  • Reachability accuracy split by observed versus inferred pairs. The ALPINE gap; a transitivity axiom in the seed either closes it or does not [12].
  • Myhill–Nerode compression and distinction. Do two seeds that induce the same structure get the same continuations, and different structures different ones? This is the test that exposed the incoherent Manhattan map [17].
  • Probe recovery of B, its closure, and the type vector. Linear first, then nonlinear; report the layer where each first becomes decodable.
  • Frame-composition error. Predicted versus true disagreement after composing restriction maps along a path.
  • Arm A versus Arm B at matched accuracy. Tokens, latency, and the failure modes of each; this is the cost of keeping the graph inside.

Support for the thesis looks like: Arm A within a few points of Arm B on inferred reachability, with B and its closure linearly decodable after the seed is gone, and the gap to runtime ④ shrinking as the curriculum lengthens. Refutation looks like: Arm A matching Arm B only on observed pairs, probes recovering B but never its closure, and accuracy collapsing with hyperedge count no matter the model size, the pattern [13] already reports for plain graphs. Either outcome is publishable; only the first would make "agent and graph, one and the same" a statement about inference rather than architecture.

Part 9

What this evidence does not establish

  • That structure found by a probe is structure the model uses. Othello and the belief-simplex work include causal interventions; most other probing results do not, and the Manhattan rejoinder shows how far behaviour underdetermines mechanism [38].
  • That any of the from-scratch results transfer to frontier models. Othello-GPT, Coconut, the belief-geometry and search papers all run at GPT-2 scale or below [13][14][18].
  • That the external-graph numbers are as large as they look. The GraphRAG evaluation methodology has a documented judge bias of over 30 points [40], and the memory-graph vendors' benchmark rankings are contradicted by independent 2026 evaluations [28].
  • That category and sheaf theory describe what pretrained transformers do. Today they describe what one can build [7].
  • That "no natural language" has been achieved end to end anywhere except formal mathematics, where a human still writes the theorem statement [20].

The honest summary: the operator is a graph (proved), the latent space can hold graphs (shown, at small scale, imperfectly), and whether a graph handed over as pure structure can be held and used within one round is unknown and, as of this writing, unattempted in the form described here.

10.3Where the industry is not better

Two places, and they are worth defending.

The single round survives its own critique. The obvious reading of context rot is "decompose into many calls". The evidence does not support that reading. A Microsoft and Salesforce result, reported in the standard taxonomy of context failure modes, found a 39% quality drop when one prompt was split into sequential turns, because early incomplete attempts stay in the context and poison the final reasoning [58]. Context poisoning, distraction, confusion and clash are all failure modes of accumulated context, which multi-turn decomposition makes worse. So: keep one round, and fix the shape of what it emits. That is what Part 12 does.

Read-once-then-destroy is unoccupied. Across everything surveyed here, no system hands a model a formal structure, destroys it, and then measures what the round retained. Memory systems persist; RAG systems retrieve; self-evolving agents archive. The destruction step is the novel experimental move, and it is the one that makes the "same and one" claim falsifiable instead of rhetorical.

The claim worth defending

A single inference round, seeded once with a typed hypergraph and an energy and nothing else, can maintain the ontology and topology it reasons over to within n points of a system that keeps that graph in an external store, at a fraction of the tokens, and the structure is linearly decodable from the residual stream after the seed is destroyed.

Part 12

Making agent and graph the same thing, better than either the papers or the frameworks

Six substitutions. Each keeps what the engine already gets right, takes the measured win from the work that beats it, and lands somewhere neither currently occupies. The result is not a bigger architecture; it is roughly the same 1500 lines with different contents.

M1Make the round a fixed point, not a pipeline

Ht+1 = Φ(Ht, ot),   yt+1 = Ψ(yt, Ht+1),   halt when E(Ht+1) − E(Ht) > −ε

The six thought maps become two operators applied repeatedly: Φ updates the structure, Ψ updates the answer. This is TRM's loop with a typed hypergraph in place of the latent vector [55], and ReLIT is the published template for attaching such a block to a frozen frontier model [56]. Supervise every step, not just the last: the ARC Prize analysis found deep supervision was the primary driver of TRM's gains, worth twice what the recursion itself was worth [55]. The engine currently gets one supervised step per round.

M2One Signature per rank, not one Signature six deep

Keep the single round; change the shape of the emission. A combinatorial complex has ranks: nodes, hyperedges, and relations among hyperedges [9]. Emit one flat structure per rank instead of one structure nested six levels. Three shallow outputs in sequence inside the same round beat one deep one on the failure mode that is best measured, since coherent deep structure is exactly what degrades attention worst [48], while avoiding the 39% penalty that comes from splitting a prompt across turns [58].

The payoff is conceptual as well as practical: the call schedule becomes the message-passing schedule. Rank 0 aggregates into hyperedges, rank 1 aggregates across them, rank 2 closes. At that point "agent is graph" is not an analogy about attention; it is a property of your own control flow, which is the strongest form of C1 any implementation can have.

M3Seed mathematics, induce the rest, never author an ontology

Keep mission.yaml and the read-once-then-destroy lifecycle; replace its prose with the seed from Part 7: incidence matrix, type symbols, restriction maps, energy, constraint formulas. After the seed is destroyed, induce new types the way AutoSchemaKG does, but incrementally, inside the round, which is precisely the limitation its own authors and later work identify in it: schema induction today is a one-time, batch, LLM-heavy pass [51]. Beating a 95%-alignment result is not the goal. Doing it continuously, at round granularity, from a mathematical seed rather than text, is a different claim and an unmade one.

M4Put GEPA on the graph, not on the prompt

Delete the Q-table. The finally block already ships every round's output, errors, logs and timing to Phoenix, which is exactly the trajectory corpus GEPA reflects over to get its 6-to-20-point gain at 35× fewer rollouts [47]. Then go one step past GEPA: reflect on the edges, not only the instructions. GPTSwarm optimises node prompts and edge connectivity separately [29]; MaAS keeps a distribution over architectures and samples per query [49]. Neither applies language-based reflection to the topology itself. Reflective edge evolution over a per-query topology distribution is, as far as this survey found, unoccupied.

M5Let the energy decide how long to think

E(H) = Σe∈E ‖δe x‖² + λ Σc∈C 𝟙[c violated] + μ |E|

First term: sheaf disagreement across hyperedges, the quantity sheaf diffusion minimises [6]. Second: constraint violations from the seed. Third: a complexity penalty so the graph cannot buy consistency by adding edges. The round iterates M1 until the energy plateaus, which gives per-query adaptive compute with no controller network to train — MaAS gets its 6-to-45% cost reduction from a learned controller [49]; an energy gauge is free and interpretable. It also gives the system something no reported agent harness has: a scalar it can be wrong about, measurable against ground-truth structure.

M6Replace the backup with an acceptance test and an archive

Self-modification needs a decision rule, not an undo button. Keep an archive of accepted variants rather than a single lineage, as the Darwin Gödel Machine does across 80 iterations with staged evaluation, and gate each edit on an anytime-valid test so you can stop early without inflating false positives [52][53]. The literature is explicit that every prior self-evolving system selects by empirical score, and that objective hacking and self-modification instability are open problems [53]. With M5 in place you have an unusually good acceptance statistic available: accept an edit when it lowers the consistency energy on held-out seeds, which is harder to game than a task score.

Part 13

The Signature is the graph

The engine's whole design surface is one DSPy Signature: an input, a docstring, and a nested output model [69]. Before asking what the number of fields does to quality, it helps to see that the output model already is a graph, and that the model's job each round is to realise it.

Eq. 8 · an output schema as a typed hypergraph skeleton
schema O = (F, ⊏, τ, δ, κ): fields F, nesting order ⊏, type map τ : F → stalks, description map δ : F → text, constraint set κ
a field of type list[R] is a hyperedge template of open arity; an id reference inside R is a non-tree edge
the emission is a realisation h : F → values, written in order ⊏, so generation order = computation order

Read against Part 3: nesting is rank, the description on a field is its restriction map (what this node may contain and how it must agree with its neighbours), the type is its stalk, and validators are the constraint term of the energy. A list of typed records with id references is a hypergraph; a six-deep nested object is a tree, which is the one graph shape that cannot express the engine's own thesis, because trees have no cycles, no shared nodes and no arity beyond parent-child.

The line that matters most is the last one in Eq. 8. A decoder-only model writes the fields in the order they are declared, and under constrained decoding it cannot do otherwise: the grammar compiles property order into a state machine, because tracking arbitrary order needs 2k states for k required keys [63]. A field declared before the answer is scratchpad the answer can use; a field declared after it is a rationalisation that cannot influence anything. The schema is not a data shape. It is the algorithm, written as a type.

Part 15

Letting the model decide ontology, topology and the actor layer at runtime, with a seeded core

The thesis in its final form: we do not decide the ontology, the topology, or the anthropology. The model decides them during the round and may change them at any time. A small set of nodes and edges is mandatory and seeded; everything else is the model's. Here "anthropology" is taken to mean the actor layer: who the people, agents and roles in the graph are, and how they stand to one another, including the model's own role. It is a third rank, not a metaphor.

Eq. 9 · seeded core, free periphery
Ht = K ∪ Ft,   K fixed (mandatory nodes, edges, types),   Ft free and rewritten by the round
Tt = TK ∪ Ttminted, each minted type carrying a definition and a nearest existing type (canonicalisation)
E(H) = Σe ‖δe x‖² + λ Σc∈CF 𝟙[c violated] + μ |F| + ∞ · 𝟙[K ⊄ H]

The last term is the whole governance model in one symbol. Removing or retyping a seeded element costs infinite energy, so the gates reject it before any score is computed; everything else is a soft cost the model trades off. The model is free to mint a type, but Eq. 9 makes it pay for the mint (the μ term) and forces it to say what the new type is nearest to, which is exactly the Define and Canonicalize steps of EDC [65] moved inside the round.

None of this is speculative at the memory layer. Graphiti describes its ontology as "prescribed and learned": types declared up front as Pydantic models, with structure otherwise emerging from the data; Zep's strict-ontology switch restricts extraction to the declared types, and each node carries exactly one type [66]. EDC does the same for extraction, with self-generated schemas when none is given [65], and AutoSchemaKG shows the free end of the dial reaching 95% alignment with expert schemas at web scale [51]. What is not published is the dial set at the round: a seeded K, a free F, and a model that rewrites F in the same emission that used it. That remains the claim.

    energy: EnergyReport                # ΔE, violations
1Order is the algorithm. Free reasoning, then observations, then the ontology delta, then nodes, edges, closure, actors, answer, energy. Each field can only use what is above it, so the schema enforces the message-passing schedule of Part 12 (lever 1).
2Depth two, never six. Nine top-level fields, each a scalar or a flat list of records. Ids carry every cross-reference, so the instance is a hypergraph while the schema stays inside every engine's coverage (levers 4 and 5).
3Descriptions are restriction maps. Each one says how a value must agree with its neighbours, not what the field is called. Optional means nullable, so required cannot manufacture a node (lever 6).
4Minting is allowed and priced. A new type or relation must appear in definitions with its nearest neighbour; the energy's μ term charges for it; the gates canonicalise against the archive after the round.
5The seed is input, not output. Mandatory ids live in the seed and are enforced by the gates, never by asking the model to repeat them. The model cannot delete what it was never asked to emit.
6Iterate, do not nest. Run the Signature again on its own output until ΔE plateaus (Part 12, M1 and M5). Depth that the schema refuses is recovered as rounds, which is where TRM's gains came from [55].
Part 16

Can tweaking the fields give an intelligent graph? The answer and the sweep that proves it

Yes to the shape, no to the substance, and the distinction is useful. Field order, depth and attributes control the shape and sequence of the computation, and on the evidence that is worth tens of points. They do not supply what the probing studies found missing: transitive closure the model never observed [12], a map that stays coherent under detours [17], search that scales with graph size [13]. Those come, if they come, from iteration with an energy stop, from supervision at every step, and from a seeded core that gives the free periphery something fixed to be consistent with. The Signature gets the model to the starting line in the right posture. It does not run the race.
